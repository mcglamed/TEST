Phase velocity describes the speed of a point of constant phase, such as a carrier-wave crest: vₚ = ω/k.

Group velocity describes the speed of the wave-packet envelope: vg = dω/dk.
Show mathematical details

Single traveling wave

ψ(x,t) = A cos(kx − ωt)

A point of constant phase satisfies kx − ωt = constant. Therefore:

vₚ = dx/dt = ω/k

Wave packet

ψ(x,t) = Σ A(kn) cos[knx − ω(kn)t]

When the component wave numbers occupy a narrow range around k₀, the envelope approximately travels at:

vg = dω/dk evaluated at k = k₀

Nondispersive relation

ω = ck,    vₚ = c,    vg = c

Quadratic relation

ω = αk²,    vₚ = αk,    vg = 2αk

The quadratic model has the same mathematical form as the dispersion relation for a nonrelativistic free-particle matter wave. Because the relation is nonlinear, different wave-number components accumulate phase at different rates, and the packet spreads.

Questions to Consider

  1. In the nondispersive case, how do the phase and group velocities compare?
  2. In the quadratic case, which moves faster: carrier crests or the packet envelope?
  3. Why does the Gaussian packet retain its shape in the nondispersive case?
  4. Why does the packet spread when the dispersion relation is quadratic?
  5. What happens to the initial spatial width when Δk is increased?
  6. Which velocity appears to describe the motion of |ψ(x,t)|²?
